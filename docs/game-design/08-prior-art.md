# Prior Art — Is the Frozen-Age Mechanic Actually New?

Claim under test: *"your capacities are frozen at the ages you were hurt, and the game lets you feel it from the inside — nobody has built that."* Verdict after a search of the landscape (July 2026): **every ingredient exists somewhere, but no found game combines them** — capacity-frozen-at-the-age-of-the-wound, expressed as domain-specific verb regression, healed by readiness-gated memory reprocessing, inside a generational perception-driven life sim. The synthesis appears unbuilt. Honest caveats at the end.

## The ingredients, and who has built each one

**Trauma as explorable content** — well-trodden ground, and the strongest tonal comparisons:
- *Omori* — repressed trauma as a literal alternate dream-world; choices lead to overcoming or suppressing the repressed memory. The closest thematic sibling. But trauma is the *plot*; it doesn't arrest the character's capacities mechanically ([Omori — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omori_(video_game)), [academic study of its environmental trauma design](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15554120231162982)).
- *Psychonauts 2* — dives into minds, with **Memory Vaults**: repressed memories distorted by time, trauma, and denial ([Memory Vaults](https://psychonauts.fandom.com/wiki/Memory_Vaults)). Memory-diving as level design, not as the player's own healing loop.
- *Celeste* — anxiety/depression manifested as the mountain and as Badeline, the rejected part of the self; resolution is integration, not banishment ([overview](https://mindowl.org/8-outstanding-video-games-that-tackle-mental-health/)). The "accept your Shadow, don't defeat it" arc — also the entire Persona series' Jungian Shadow-acceptance scene ("I am thou…"). Our pillar 2 has strong precedent; the frozen-age mechanic does not.

**Mental state constraining player verbs** — the nearest *mechanical* cousins:
- *Depression Quest* — choices are shown but crossed out, unavailable because of the character's depression. Verb-limitation-by-mental-state, the single closest existing mechanic to our regression idea — but it's global and static (depression removes options everywhere), not *domain-specific and age-shaped* (a fourteen-year-old's option set appearing only in trust situations).
- *Disco Elysium* — 24 skills as literal voices/parts of the psyche arguing in dialogue; mental state, internalized thoughts, even clothes modify what you can say and do ([skills design](https://discoelysium.wiki.gg/wiki/Skills), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_Elysium)). The best parts-work game ever made — but the parts are *aptitudes you build up*, not capacities frozen at wound-ages, and there's no development-arrest or unfreezing structure.

**Memory revisiting as healing** — exists in scattered forms:
- *To the Moon* — traversing and altering a dying man's memories (as external doctors, not as self-reprocessing).
- *TRAUMA* (indie, itch.io) — navigating a woman's psyche in therapy sessions through photographs of memory ([itch page](https://uppon-hill.itch.io/trauma)).
- *Luna* (VR) — unscrambling memories to rebuild a shattered world ([Voices of VR](https://voicesofvr.com/438-luna-a-deep-game-narrative-puzzler-about-recovering-from-grief-trauma/)).
- None gate the revisiting behind present-day earned safety, and none unfreeze a capacity that then *resumes growing* — the readiness→reprocess→regrow loop has no found precedent.

**Emotional regression under stress** — exists as a narrative trope (TV Tropes catalogs it as [Emotional Regression](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/EmotionalRegression): characters temporarily reverting to childlike selves when triggered), and psychology sources describe the real phenomenon of being [emotionally stuck at the age of trauma](https://psychcentral.com/ptsd/signs-trauma-has-you-stuck). But in games it's cutscene characterization, not a player-facing system — no found game hands the *player* an age-regressed verb set in triggering domains.

**The adjacent systems in our design also have (separate) precedents worth knowing:** world-color/vibrancy as emotional state (*Chicory*, *Concrete Genie*, *GRIS*); small-town relationship webs over time (*Night in the Woods*, *Mutazione*); generational persistence and becoming another person at death (*One Hour, One Life*; *Crusader Kings*; the 1986 life-sim *Alter Ego* for birth-to-death psychological choice); grief-processing structure (*Spiritfarer*). Useful references, none combining with the above.

## The claim, refined

What's defensibly novel is the **synthesis**: (1) capacities frozen at the age of the wound, (2) surfaced as *domain-specific verb regression* the player feels from the inside rather than reads about, (3) with Shadow thickness = the age gap, compounding by simply living, (4) healed through readiness-gated memory reprocessing that *resumes* growth rather than granting it, (5) inside a perception-rendered generational town. Individual pieces: Depression Quest (verb gating), Disco Elysium (parts-as-psyche), Omori (repression as world), Psychonauts (memory vaults), Chicory (world-as-feeling). The assembled machine: no found precedent.

## Honest caveats

- Web search is shallow for unreleased/small games: itch.io jams, therapy-adjacent serious games, and non-English titles are underindexed. "No found precedent" is not "provably none."
- Do a proper competitive scan (Steam upcoming, itch.io tags like *mental-health* / *inner-child*, IGF entrant lists) before using the novelty claim in any pitch material.
- Novelty is an argument for attention, not for quality — Depression Quest proves the mechanic-shape can work; Disco Elysium proves the audience exists; neither proves *this* combination lands. That's what Phase 0 is for.
